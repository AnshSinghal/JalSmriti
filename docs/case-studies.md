# Before the flood: 16 places, read before they flooded

These are 16 places in India and Nepal that flooded between 2020 and 2026, or where families lost homes built on old lake
land. We checked each one with JalSmriti as it runs today, on the live deployment, on 11 October 2026. The same results
are on the website's **Past floods** page (`/proof`), and each place check can be opened there.

**What this is, and what it is not.** JalSmriti's water history ends in 2020, and the terrain does not change with the
weather. So these readings show what a family could have seen before most of these floods. They do not prove that
anyone would have been saved. A fair test also needs places that did not flood, which is why the comparison below is
included.

## Summary

- All 16 places show at least one warning sign, such as old lake beds built over nearby, a hollow in the
  ground, low ground near a drain, or radar sightings.
- Water memory: 3 strong, 11 some, 2 little.
- Verdict for a family renting a ground-floor flat: 1 "Look elsewhere", 13 "Think twice", 2 "Fine, with checks" and 0 "Good to go".
- Verdict for a buyer of a ground-floor flat with a car in basement parking: 11 "Look elsewhere" and 5 "Think twice".

### The same verdict on the validation set

This uses the localities the water memory index was checked against: 39 places reported flooded in Bengaluru, Chennai,
Hyderabad and Delhi-Gurugram, and 240 random built-up places in the same cities, each at least 1 km from every flooded
one. Radar is left out, so this is satellite history and terrain alone.

| Places | Look elsewhere | Think twice | Fine, with checks | Good to go | Warned (look elsewhere or think twice) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reported flooded (39) | 6 | 26 | 6 | 1 | 82% |
| Random built-up (240) | 12 | 114 | 52 | 62 | 53% |

The verdict warns at about 1.6 times the rate on places that flooded. "Look elsewhere" is about three times as common
there (15% against 5%). It also warns at about half of ordinary built-up places, because much of these cities sits in
valleys and on old tank beds. So a warning means "check before you sign", not "never live here".

## What would have changed

1. **Before signing.** A clear read in plain words, with the questions to ask: take the first floor, skip basement
   parking, ask about the last floods.
2. **Before the rain.** An email heads-up for any place you follow, with a lower threshold where the ground remembers
   water. Velachery residents said in 2023 that "this time there was no warning".
3. **Before buying.** A drafted Right to Information request for the lake's survey map, full tank level and list of
   encroachments, so the buffer zone is checked before any money is paid.
4. **For the lake itself.** Photos, signed dossiers and RTI filings go into a tamper-evident record, so encroachment is
   reported while it can still be stopped.

## Limits

- These places were chosen because they flooded. The comparison with random built-up places above is the fair test.
- Radar saw standing water only at Yamuna Bazar. City floods drain within hours, and for most of 2022 to 2025 only one
  Sentinel-1 satellite was working. No radar sighting does not mean a place is safe.
- Satellite pixels are 10 to 30 m, and outlines are approximate: this is not a land survey.
- Case details come from news reports, court and audit findings. Where the figures conflict, the case says so.

## Why it matters

- Bengaluru lost 79% of its water bodies between 1973 and 2017 (IISc, via [Mongabay](https://www.thequint.com/amp/story/climate-change/as-bengaluru-loses-lakes-green-cover-impact-of-urban-floods-takes-a-toll)).
- 390 million Indians are directly exposed to 1-in-100-year floods ([Rentschler et al., Nature Communications 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-30727-4)).
- Rain floods in Indian cities cost about US$4 billion a year ([World Bank, Jul 2025](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2025/07/22/india-has-a-critical-opportunity-to-drive-resilient-urban-development-says-new-world-bank-report)).
- 24 hours' warning can cut flood damage by 30% ([Global Commission on Adaptation, via WMO](https://wmo.int/node/13544)).
- Odisha's 1999 super cyclone killed more than 10,000 people; the equally strong Cyclone Phailin in 2013, with warnings and evacuation, killed about 45 ([WMO via PreventionWeb](https://www.preventionweb.net/quick/21624)).
